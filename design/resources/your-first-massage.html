The honest picture

What massage can and can’t do

We’d rather you arrive with accurate expectations than big promises. Here’s what the research supports, in plain language.

What the research suggests

Serious problems are rare

The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) describes the risk of harm from massage as low. The rare serious problems reported have mostly involved vigorous work or people at higher risk of injury.1 That’s why we ask about your health first and adapt the pressure to you.

Evidence on pain is still developing

Research suggests massage may help neck and shoulder pain, though the benefit may be short-lived, and some fibromyalgia symptoms when it’s continued long enough. For low-back pain, headaches and other conditions, the evidence is weaker or mixed.1

You don’t need a problem to fix

Many people book massage to feel more comfortable in their body, to unwind, or to support recovery from training and physical work. Those are good reasons on their own.

What massage isn’t

A substitute for medical care

Massage doesn’t diagnose or treat medical conditions. If something feels wrong, see your healthcare provider rather than waiting for a massage to sort it out.1

A detox

You may hear that massage “flushes toxins.” There’s no good evidence for it; your liver and kidneys already do that job. More on page 8.

A test of toughness

More pressure doesn’t mean more benefit, and you never have to grit your teeth through a session. More on pages 6 and 7.

How we think about massage

Restore capacity so the body has more room to move, recover and respond.

Massage & Bodywork at TAB supports comfort, movement, recovery, body awareness and physical adaptability. When medical circumstances are involved, we work within professional scope and may ask for medical clearance, or coordinate with your healthcare team, before we begin.

Before you book

A little preparation goes a long way

Choosing a practitioner

In Michigan, massage therapists must be licensed by the state, and you can look up a license on the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA) website.2 It’s also fair to ask about a practitioner’s training and experience, and the kinds of work they do most.1

Pregnant?

Prenatal massage is adapted to each stage of pregnancy, with side-lying positions and supportive cushions so you can rest comfortably. Let us know how far along you are when you book.

What to tell us

What brings you in

A tight neck, training recovery, stress, or plain curiosity. Any reason is a good reason.

Your health

Conditions, recent injuries or surgery, medications, and pregnancy. We go over this privately at your visit.

What to avoid

Areas you’d rather we didn’t work on, ticklish spots, or anything that felt wrong in a past massage.

Sensitivities

Allergies or sensitivities to oils, lotions or scents. Tell us about any rash, cut or skin infection, too.

Your preferences

Pressure, temperature, music, and whether you’d like to talk or stay quiet.

Your goals

What you’d like to feel or do afterwards. “Feel less stiff at my desk” is a perfectly good goal.

Please don’t email us health details. We’ll ask about them privately, in person, when you arrive.

Check with your healthcare provider first if you:

  • have had surgery recently
  • are being treated for cancer
  • have, or have had, a blood clot, or take blood-thinning medication
  • have osteoporosis, a recent fracture or a recent significant injury
  • are managing a serious or changing health condition

We may ask for medical clearance before your first session. It isn’t a hurdle; it’s how we make sure the work is right for you.

Feeling unwell on the day? If you have a fever or something contagious, call us. Your massage can wait until you’re better.

On the table

Comfort, privacy and pressure

The details people wonder about but don’t always ask.

What to wear

Undress only as far as you’re comfortable. Many people keep their underwear on, and that’s completely fine. Some techniques work well over comfortable clothing; we’ll tell you if that’s the case.

Staying covered

A sheet covers you throughout. Only the area being worked on is uncovered, then covered again. Ask any time if you’d like it adjusted.

Positioning

You may start face down, with your face in a padded cradle, or face up. Side-lying with cushions is an option too, and the usual position in pregnancy. Too warm, too cool, need another pillow? Just say.

Talking

Chat or stay quiet: both are fine. Many people talk at first and drift into quiet, and some drift toward sleep. We’ll still check in about pressure.

Finding the right pressure

Speak up

You’re in control, the whole time

A good session is a conversation, even when it’s a quiet one. Your feedback is how we get it right.

How should it feel?

Comfortable

Feels good. You can breathe easily and stay relaxed. Keep going.

Intense, but OK

A strong sensation that eases as we work. You can still breathe slowly and let go. Tell us if it changes.

Too much

You hold your breath, tense up or pull away. That’s your signal to say so, and we’ll ease off straight away.

Things you can say

None of these will surprise us. We’d much rather hear them than have you put up with something.

“Could you use a little less pressure there?”
“That’s a good spot. Can you stay there a bit longer?”
“I’m a little cold.”
“I’d rather you didn’t work on my feet.”Or face, stomach, or anywhere else.
“I’d like to keep quiet today.”
“What are you noticing in that area?”

Pause, change or stop, any time.

You can pause, change or stop the session at any time, for any reason, and you never need to explain. The same goes for any area you’d rather we left alone.

Myths and facts

Common myths, and what’s true

A few ideas about massage that are worth retiring.

You may have heardWhat’s true
“It has to hurt to work.”More pressure isn’t more benefit.Your body responds best to pressure it can accept without bracing. The rare serious problems linked to massage have mostly involved vigorous work.1
“Massage flushes toxins out of your muscles.”There’s no good evidence for this.Your liver and kidneys clear waste, and one study found massage actually slowed the clearing of lactate from exercised muscle.3 Water afterwards is still sensible: you’ve been lying still for a while.
“I need to know which technique to ask for.”You don’t.Most sessions blend several techniques. Tell us how you feel and what you’d like to be different, and we’ll choose and adjust as we go.
“I’ll have to undress completely.”Only as far as you’re comfortable.You stay covered except for the area being worked on, and some techniques are done fully clothed.
“Falling asleep, or a rumbling stomach, would be embarrassing.”Both happen often, and nobody minds.Neither do unshaved legs. There’s nothing you need to do to get your body “ready.” Come as you are.
“Massage is a luxury, or only for athletes.”People book for all kinds of reasons.Everyday tension, training recovery, pregnancy, changing health and simple comfort, at every stage of adult life and every fitness level.

After your session

What to notice, and what helps

The hour on the table is only part of it. What you notice over the next day or two helps shape what comes next.

What’s normal

  • Feeling relaxed or sleepy, or a little light-headed when you first sit up. Sit up slowly and take a moment.
  • Some tenderness for a day or two, especially after firmer work, much like after a new workout.
  • Noticing areas you hadn’t paid attention to before. That’s body awareness, and it’s useful.

What helps

  • Drink water and eat as you normally would.
  • Move gently: a walk or some easy stretching.
  • Notice how you move, sit and feel, and tell us next time.
  • Training hard soon? Tell us when you book, so we can time the work around it.

Call your healthcare provider if you notice

  • pain that gets worse rather than easing after a couple of days
  • new swelling, warmth or redness in an arm or leg
  • numbness or tingling that doesn’t settle
  • anything that worries you

Then let us know too, so we can adapt your next session.

How often should I come?

It depends on what you’re working on. We’ll suggest a rhythm that fits your goals and your schedule, never more than you need.

The aim is more capacity on your own terms: more room to move, recover and respond in daily life, not dependence on the table.

Build ownership, not dependence

Massage connects with Functional Strength, Women’s Wellness, Integrated Nutrition and Holistic Life Coaching when that genuinely supports your goals, and only then. Plenty of people simply book a massage, for as long as it helps.

Where massage fits: Transform. Adapt. Balance.

Transform

Address tension, restrictions and recovery needs.

Adapt

Improve the body’s ability to respond and recover.

Balance

Recalibrate effort, restoration and physical demand.
