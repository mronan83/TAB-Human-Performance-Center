# Executive Summary

Step 19 operationalizes the rebrand across every client-facing and internal touchpoint. The plan uses a phased migration so that the new brand can launch cleanly without sacrificing the digital and relationship equity accumulated under TAB Massage & Fitness.

## The five-phase transition model

| **Phase** | **Purpose** | **Typical timing** |
|----|----|----|
| 0 — Clearance & Ownership | Complete naming/legal checks, secure domains/handles, inventory systems and confirm account control. | Before irreversible spend |
| 1 — Build Behind the Scenes | Prepare website, systems, forms, collateral, redirects and communications without public cutover. | T-8 to T-4 weeks |
| 2 — Pre-Launch Communication | Prepare clients, partners and team before they encounter the new identity. | T-3 to T-1 weeks |
| 3 — Launch / Cutover | Coordinate website, Google, Timely, Stripe, email, social, signage and internal changes. | Launch day |
| 4–5 — Transition to Steady State | Monitor, reduce confusion, preserve legacy pathways and gradually retire “formerly” language. | Launch to +6 months |

# 19.1 — Rebrand Transition Strategy

## The transition story

> TAB Massage & Fitness is becoming TAB Wellness Collective.

The public explanation should stay simple: the name is evolving to better reflect the broader, whole-person approach TAB provides today — Massage & Bodywork, Personal Strength Training, Women’s Wellness Coaching and Holistic Life Coaching.

> Same commitment. Broader expression of what TAB has become.

## Transition naming standard

- Permanent brand: TAB Wellness Collective.

- Philosophy: Transform. Align. Become.

- Temporary transition phrase: “formerly TAB Massage & Fitness.”

- Do not place “formerly…” inside the permanent logo or wordmark.

- Use the transition phrase selectively for approximately 90–180 days where recognition matters.

# 19.2 — Detailed Transition Phases

## Phase 0 — Clearance & Ownership

- Complete formal trademark clearance and Michigan entity/assumed-name work.

- Secure the primary domain and relevant defensive domains.

- Reserve preferred social handles.

- Confirm administrative access to website/DNS, Google Business Profile, Search Console, Analytics, Timely, Stripe, email and social accounts.

- Inventory all current brand touchpoints, vendor accounts, printed materials and recurring templates.

- Preserve current website analytics, search performance, page inventory and backlink data.

## Phase 1 — Build Behind the Scenes

- Build and QA the new public website on the new domain.

- Prepare My TAB architecture or a clear interim Client Login experience.

- Configure new email aliases/addresses while preserving old ones.

- Prepare Timely branding and booking-link changes.

- Prepare Stripe customer-facing naming, receipts and portal branding.

- Stage Google Business Profile assets and text.

- Prepare social profile art, bios and launch posts.

- Order signage and printed collateral.

- Update forms, consent materials, client-facing PDFs and templates.

- Build the old-to-new URL redirect map and SEO migration plan.

- Prepare staff scripts and client/partner communications.

## Phase 2 — Pre-Launch Communication

Begin communicating approximately 2–3 weeks before launch. Existing clients should never encounter the new brand unexpectedly.

- Explain what is changing: name, visual identity, website and expanded expression of services.

- Explain why: the new brand better represents TAB’s whole-person approach.

- Explain what is not changing: appointments, practitioners, client history, relationships and commitment to personalized care.

- Clarify that clients should not need to take action unless specifically notified.

- Introduce “Transform. Align. Become.” and the four service pillars.

## Phase 3 — Launch / Cutover

- Make TAB Wellness Collective the public name across the website, signage and key digital profiles.

- Publish the new website and enable permanent redirects from legacy URLs.

- Update Timely business name, branding, location details, links and templates.

- Update Stripe customer-facing branding, support details, receipt/invoice context and statement descriptor if required.

- Update email signatures and activate the new-domain addresses/aliases.

- Update Google Business Profile once the public website/signage supports the new real-world identity.

- Update social display names, handles, artwork, bios and links.

- Notify partners and publish the launch announcement.

- Run end-to-end smoke testing on booking, forms, email and payment paths.

## Phase 4 — Transition Period

For roughly the first 90 days, use “TAB Wellness Collective — formerly TAB Massage & Fitness” where it materially reduces confusion. Monitor search, booking and client behavior closely.

## Phase 5 — Steady State

At roughly +3 to +6 months, TAB Wellness Collective should stand on its own. Legacy naming remains only where historically or operationally useful — redirects, old email aliases, the Our Story narrative and legal records.

# 19.3 — Website & Domain Migration

The domain migration is a major equity-preservation activity. The goal is to move to the new brand domain without throwing away the history and referral traffic attached to tabmassage.com.

## Domain strategy

- Primary destination: tabwellnesscollective.com, subject to final availability/clearance.

- Retain tabmassage.com indefinitely if renewal cost remains nominal.

- Use server-side 301/308 permanent redirects from each meaningful old URL to its closest new equivalent.

- Do not redirect every old page blindly to the homepage.

- Retain legacy email-domain aliases in parallel with the website redirects.

| **Legacy URL example**  | **Recommended destination**                     |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| tabmassage.com/         | tabwellnesscollective.com/                      |
| /massage                | /services/massage-bodywork                      |
| /personal-training      | /services/personal-strength-training            |
| /about                  | /about                                          |
| Legacy article/resource | Matching new /resources article where available |

## Launch SEO checklist

- Build and test the full redirect map before launch.

- Avoid redirect chains and redirect loops.

- Update internal links, canonical tags and XML sitemap.

- Review robots.txt and remove any staging noindex directives.

- Verify both domains in Google Search Console.

- Submit the new sitemap and domain move/change-of-address workflow where applicable.

- Update analytics/measurement configuration.

- Crawl the legacy site and new site for 404s before and after launch.

- Update major external backlinks and directory links where feasible.

- Expect temporary search fluctuation while the new URLs are reprocessed.

# 19.4 — Google Business Profile & Local Search

## Recommended sequence

## NAP consistency

# 19.6 — Stripe & Financial Experience

Stripe remains the payment and financial-transaction system of record. The transition should update customer-facing branding while preserving transaction history and avoiding any need to move card data into TAB systems.

- Update public/business name where appropriate.

- Update support email and support URL.

- Refresh receipt, invoice and Customer Portal branding.

- Review subscription and product names.

- Review payment links and checkout descriptions.

- Define and document the card-statement descriptor.

- Keep card/bank information in Stripe rather than My TAB.

## Statement descriptor

Because the full public name may exceed processor descriptor limits, use a recognizable abbreviated financial descriptor such as TABWELLNESSCOLL rather than turning “TAB Wellness” into a secondary marketing brand.

# 19.7 — Email & Domain Migration

- Create new-domain email addresses/aliases before launch.

- Use role addresses such as hello@, support@ and billing@ where useful.

- Preserve all legacy @tabmassage.com addresses as aliases/forwarders for years, potentially indefinitely.

- Use a 90-day transitional signature that includes “formerly TAB Massage & Fitness.”

- Avoid permanent auto-replies on legacy addresses; if used at launch, keep them short and temporary.

> You’re in the right place — TAB Massage & Fitness is now TAB Wellness Collective. Your message has reached us normally.

# 19.8 — Social Media Transition

Keep existing accounts wherever possible. Rebranding in place preserves followers, engagement history, reviews and social proof.

- Change display name and handle when available.

- Update profile icon and cover/banner.

- Update bio, website and booking links.

- Pin a launch/rebrand announcement.

- Use “formerly TAB Massage & Fitness” for approximately 60–90 days where helpful.

- After transition, simplify the bio around whole-person wellness and Transform. Align. Become.

## Suggested launch-post structure

> TAB is evolving. TAB Massage & Fitness is now TAB Wellness Collective.

Then explain that the new name reflects the broader whole-person model and explicitly list the four service pillars before reinforcing continuity.

# 19.14 — My TAB Transition

My TAB does not have to launch on the same day as the public rebrand. Do not delay the whole brand transition solely because the client portal is still in development.

- If My TAB is ready: integrate it into the launch as the authenticated client experience.

- If My TAB is not ready: continue using a straightforward “Client Login” path into Timely.

- Only publish “My TAB — coming soon” if there is a credible delivery roadmap and the message will not linger indefinitely.

- Introduce My TAB later as a meaningful product/experience release rather than an empty placeholder.
